Reflection on Static vs. Interactive Visualizations

A static visualization, like the matplotlib bar and line charts I made for tasks 1
and 2, is basically a picture. Whatever you decided to show is what the viewer gets,
and if they want to see something else they have to ask you to make another chart. An
interactive visualization like the Plotly scatter in task 3 is different because the
chart reacts to the person looking at it. You can hover over a point to read its exact
numbers, zoom into a crowded area, or click a direction in the legend to hide it and
focus on the rest. The static chart is great when you already know the one story you
want to tell, and the interactive one is better when you want the viewer to go dig
around and find their own answers.

Dashboards take that interactive idea further and are really useful for real-time data
exploration. Instead of running a script and saving an image every time the numbers
change, a dashboard pulls in fresh data and updates on its own, so you are always
looking at what is happening right now instead of a snapshot from this morning. You can
also put several views in one place and add filters and date ranges, which means one
person can answer a bunch of different questions without anyone re-coding a chart. For
something like watching sales come in during the day, that constant live view is much
more helpful than a fixed picture.

Tools like Plotly also just make data easier to communicate in a professional setting.
When you hand someone a static chart, they often come back with follow-up questions, but
with an interactive Plotly chart saved as an HTML file they can open it in any browser
and explore the answers themselves. The hover tooltips mean you do not have to clutter
the chart with labels, and being able to zoom and filter lets a manager check the exact
numbers behind a trend instead of just trusting the shape of the line. That builds more
confidence in the data and cuts down on the back-and-forth, which makes the whole
conversation about the numbers go faster.
